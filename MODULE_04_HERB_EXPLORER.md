# Module 04 — Herb-to-Evidence Explorer

## 1. Purpose
To create a structured digital encyclopedia of Ayurvedic medicinal plants that connects traditional knowledge, botanical identification and modern scientific research.

## 2. Main Features

1. Search herbs by common name, Sanskrit name or botanical name.
2. Display botanical identity and plant family.
3. Show plant photographs and identification information.
4. Display traditional Ayurvedic properties.
5. Provide references from classical Ayurvedic texts.
6. Display modern scientific research and publications.
7. Show relevant clinical studies where available.
8. Display known safety concerns and possible interactions.
9. Connect herbs with related Ayurvedic concepts.
10. Allow users to bookmark herbs.

## 3. Herb Information Structure

Every herb record should contain:

- Common names
- Sanskrit names
- Botanical name
- Plant family
- Plant identification
- Plant parts used
- Traditional Ayurvedic properties
- Traditional references
- Modern research findings
- Clinical research references
- Known safety information
- Potential interactions
- Original source links
- Licence and attribution details
- Verification status
- Last reviewed date

## 4. Example User Journey

A user searches for Tulsi.

The platform displays:
- Common name: Tulsi
- Sanskrit name: Tulasi
- Botanical name: Ocimum tenuiflorum
- Plant family
- Botanical description
- Traditional Ayurvedic references
- Modern scientific publications
- Available clinical studies
- Known safety information
- Source citations

## 5. Interface Requirements

- Herb search bar
- Herb category filters
- Botanical information panel
- Traditional knowledge section
- Scientific evidence section
- Safety information section
- Source reference panel
- Bookmark functionality

## 6. Evidence Classification

Clearly separate:

- Traditional textual descriptions
- Laboratory studies
- Animal studies
- Human clinical studies
- Systematic reviews and meta-analyses

Do not treat preliminary laboratory findings as proof of effectiveness in humans.

## 7. Important Rules

- Verify botanical identity.
- Avoid confusing herbs with similar common names.
- Never fabricate research citations.
- Display known safety concerns and uncertainty.
- Preserve source attribution and copyright restrictions.
- Do not provide unsupported treatment recommendations.

## 8. Initial MVP

Start with 10–20 verified medicinal plant records containing botanical identity, traditional references, scientific research and available safety information.
